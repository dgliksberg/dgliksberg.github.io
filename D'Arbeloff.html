Soft Robotic Sling

Over the summer of 2024, I interned at the d'Arbeloff robotics laboratory. I worked on redesigning the base for a soft robot sling designed to reduce the manual labor required to reposition and transport those in need of care.

The goal of the redesign was to balance the base when it's being hoisted via lift, integrate hook loops that allow for mid-operation rotation, and to significantly reduce the weight of the base.

Redesign and CAD

I started this project by fully modelling the base in CAD, down to the screw and rubber washer. The CAD would be the main assembly guide for any future intern working on the project, so I wanted it to be as accurate as possible to the physical system. The previous iteration did have a cad model, but because I redesigned so many components I decided to re-make it from scratch.

The first new components I designed for the base were the hook loops. In operation, the sling would be extended under the patient, then the lift would be clipped on to the base and sling, then the patient would be lifted. It was important that the sling come out of the base straight, so the base needed to be able to pivot while suspended to stay in line with the sling. I designed the hook loops in such a way that the weight of the patient on the sling would do this alignment automatically.

Another benefit of modelling every component of the system in CAD was that I could get a much better center of mass estimate. I used this to ensure that the center of mass of the base would be perfectly centered between the two hook loops.

I then used a simplified version of the system to perform Finite Element Analysis. I needed to ensure that none of the 3D printed parts would break, and that the base would not have any issues when brought to operating pressure.

Printing and Manufacturing

I designed all of the plastic components with 3D printing in mind, so all of the 3D printing went smoothly. The only major consideration in this application was layer orientation to maximize the structural strength of the prints. The combinaton hook loop and motor housing, for example, was printed in 3 parts to make sure that layer orientations were optimized.

The metal manufacturing, however, was a more interesting endeavor. The base had 3 aluminum components: two end plates and a large box tube extrusion that would be turned into the main body. The tube in particular was a unique challenge because of its size and this being my first time machining a component that required CNC.

After the base itself was completed, I designed control electronics that would allow for remote controlled, battery-powered operation of the sling.
