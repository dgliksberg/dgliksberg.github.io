Class Competition Team: Brave Little Toasters

For one of my classes in Spring 2025, I was team leader for the design of a mobile robot that would compete in a competition in conjunction with a team working with a robotic arm.

My team, the Brave Little Toasters, ended up winning an award for best hardware design out of the entire class for the year, and I individually was given award for being the "Most Valuable Engineer" on my team.

Project Goal and Planning

The class project began with the reveal of the game board, which was themed around plastic pollution and cleanup. The mobile robot was tasked with moving bins of plastic bottles from starting to ending positions without spilling bottles, and points were deducted based on the ammount of manual intervention required (e.g. joystick control vs. automatic action). The mobile robot team then met as a group to plan possible designs for the robot, and eventually settled on what we called a "C-Channel" design, where the bin would be fully encased in the robot during transport and would be draw in/pushed out by soft rubber flywheels.

Finalized Design

Over time, the design of the robot was refined. A limit switch was added to the back of the bin cavity so that the bin could be retained in the robot during motion via hysteresis (enabling flywheels when unpressed, locking when pressed). A tilted beam was added below the switch, which would pop the bin up on top of the flywheels after the initial intake, securing it more firmly and preventing additional friction from dragging the bin around. A lot of the beams included in the original design were also removed for weight.

Final robot and competition results

I decided to name my robot Gurt, because I found it funny. I qualified for the livestreamed competition, which was a part of the MIT Mechanical Engineering Department's 150-year anniversary, and I ended up as a quarterfinalist.
