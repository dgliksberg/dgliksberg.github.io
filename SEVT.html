Solar Electric Vehicle Rear Suspension

As subteam co-lead, I designed and conducted materials testing for a trailing suspension arm for a solar-powered electric vehicle.

  • Back

Ideation

We started the design process by considering two possible arm designs, which we called the L-Arm and the Lambda-Arm. The Lambda-Arm was the design used for the previous car, and our initial goal was to determine which arm design would give better performance in terms of force transmitted into the chassis of the car. We used MATLAB to keep track of our calculatons, and to prevent re-solving everything by hand if we decided to change an assumption or design requirement. We used the same methodology to determine the ideal spring constant, unloaded spring length, and damping constant for the shock/spring system.

Refinement

Eventually, we came to the conclusion that the L-Arm design would give us better performance in a smaller footprint. From there, the next pressing concern became material. We whittled our options down to welded metal tubing or machined aluminum. We decided to proceed forwards with both choices, and to create an arm design for each material. We would then decide on which design to go forward with after feedback from a design review with other and former team members, based on design for manufacturability, results from finite element analysis, and experience from members who worked on previous cars. We also created a 'bounding blob' for the arm to both help other subteams design around our arm and help us avoid interference with other subsystems. I was tasked with designing the steel arm, while another rear suspension team member designed the aluminum version.

Current Iteration

As of March 2025, the rear suspension subteam is working on refining the steel arm design for our next design review. We are performing materials testing with the steel tubes we intend to use on the car, which we are using validate the primary welded joint of the arm. We our narrowing down our options for off-the-shelf shocks, springs, and bearings, and aiming to further reduce the weight of the arm as much as possible.
